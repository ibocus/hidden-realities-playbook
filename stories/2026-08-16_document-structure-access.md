# Story: Where Does This Even Live?

**Theme**: Storage Consolidation

**Date Captured**: 2026-08-16

**Status**: Raw

---

## The Assumption

Once we'd agreed on a documentation language, we assumed the next step was simple: start writing. Structure could come later.

---

## The Reality

It couldn't. Before a single ISO document got written, we had to decide where these documents would actually live, and who could get to them.

First question: where's the folder structure itself? Not just "a shared drive somewhere," but a location that can genuinely be locked down. If it's SharePoint, that means a restricted SharePoint site, not a folder buried inside a general one. Access limited to specific members, not the wider organization.

Second question: within that restricted location, how do we separate work in progress from what's actually approved? We split it in two. A Draft folder, where the working versions live: half-finished procedures, redlines, whatever's still being argued over. And a Final folder, holding the approved, audit-ready version.

Third, and this one matters most: the auditor does not get access to the Draft folder. Only Final.

---

## Why It Matters

- Structure and access decisions have to come before content creation, not after.
- Draft documents (arguments, mistakes, half-formed ideas) aren't audit evidence and shouldn't be treated like they are.
- Giving an auditor visibility into a Draft folder creates confusion about what's actually in force versus still being negotiated.
- A restricted, access-controlled location is itself a control, not just good housekeeping.
- Without deciding this upfront, you either scramble to reorganize mid-project or expose more than you meant to.

---

## The Decision

1. Restricted SharePoint site (or equivalent restricted location), access limited to specific named members, not company-wide.
2. Two folders inside: Draft (working documents) and Final (approved, audit-ready documents).
3. Auditor access limited to Final only. Draft stays internal, full stop.

---

## The Lesson

Standard ISO guidance tells you what documents to produce. It says almost nothing about where they should physically live, or who should be able to open them while they're still being written.

Decide this before you start writing, not after. Two questions, answered upfront: where does this actually live, and is that location genuinely restricted; and how do we separate "still being worked on" from "this is the real, approved version," and who gets to see which.
