# Story: The Documentation Ghost

**Theme**: Discovery & Assessment → Legacy Documentation & Integration

**Date Captured**: 2026-08-15

**Status**: Ready

---

## The Assumption

We assumed our organization (200+ people, been operating 15+ years) had documented most critical business processes.

The thinking: "A company this size, this long in operation, surely they have procedures documented. Maybe not perfectly, but they're *written down somewhere*."

---

## The Reality

During the first discovery workshop with the Finance team, we asked: "Which of your critical financial controls have formal, documented procedures?"

The Finance lead went quiet. Then: "Honestly? Most of them are just... how we do it. It's not written down."

What emerged over the next few hours:
- 40% of critical financial controls lived nowhere but in people's heads
- They had spreadsheets, ad-hoc notes, and tribal knowledge
- No formal procedures. Ever.
- The team's senior accountant did most of the complex controls "because she knows how"
- New team members learned by watching, not by reading

When we pushed to see documentation: "We don't really document. We just... do it the way we've always done it."

We left that workshop realizing: this isn't unique to Finance. This is probably everywhere.

---

## Why It Matters

Without documented procedures, you cannot:

1. **Assess the control against ISO**: ISO requires evidence that the control exists and is documented
2. **Ensure consistency**: Different people do it slightly differently (or forget steps)
3. **Pass audit**: Auditors will ask: "Where's the procedure?" No document = control fails
4. **Ensure continuity**: The control is entirely dependent on one person's memory. What happens if she leaves?
5. **Hand off or train**: New team members learn by osmosis, not by reading and understanding
6. **Measure or improve**: If it's not written, you can't measure it or identify improvements

---

## The Decision

We realized we had two choices:

**Option A**: Map the control as-is (undocumented) and let Finance document it later
- Pro: Moves the timeline forward
- Con: Audit will fail on "lack of documentation"; we're setting them up to fail

**Option B**: Stop the gap analysis and start with documentation
- Pro: We document first, then assess; controls actually meet ISO requirements
- Con: Delays the timeline, costs extra effort

**We chose Option B**, but smartly:

1. **We didn't ask Finance to write fancy procedures.** We said: "Tell us how you do this; we'll help format it."
2. **We did the first 5 critical controls together** (a 4-hour workshop): I asked questions, they talked, I wrote, they reviewed.
3. **We gave them a simple template** for the remaining controls
4. **We embedded documentation into their monthly governance checklist** — not a separate project, just part of how they work
5. **We assigned clear ownership** to the Finance lead (not IT), so they own the documentation going forward

---

## The Lesson

**Discovery phase isn't "assess what you have." It's "discover what you need to document first."**

Standard guidance says: "Map controls, identify gaps, remediate." We did that in order, and it failed.

Real implementation order:
1. **Ask**: What controls do you have? How do you do them?
2. **Document**: Write down how it's actually done
3. **Assess**: Check against ISO
4. **Improve**: Close gaps

If you skip step 2, you're assessing undocumented controls. Audit will reject them.

**What we'd do differently next time:**
- Budget discovery phase as 4–6 weeks, not 2
- Assume zero existing documentation
- Build documentation as part of discovery (not after)
- Treat teams owning the documentation (not IT/security) 
- Don't separate "discovery" from "documentation" — they're the same phase

---

## Time Investment

- First workshop (discovery + template creation): 4 hours
- Follow-up interviews with Finance (guide them through 5 controls): 8 hours
- Creating templates and guidance: 6 hours
- Incorporating into governance routine: 2 hours

**Total: ~20 hours to discover and respond to this gap in one department**

---

## Context

- **Project Phase**: Assessment & Discovery (Week 2-3)
- **Teams Involved**: Finance team (5 people), ISMS team (2 people)
- **Tools Used**: Template in Word, interviews via Zoom
- **What Made It Hard**: Finance team was busy; they initially thought "we don't have time to document" until we showed them it's part of compliance, not optional
- **Stakeholder Dynamics**: Finance lead was cooperative once she understood the requirement; getting other team members to sit for interviews took follow-up

## Related Stories

- **"Format Harmonization Challenge"**: After we documented Finance procedures, we realized their format didn't match IT's or Operations' format. Triggered a whole workstream on template consistency.
- **"The Storage Consolidation Surprise"**: Once we had procedures, we discovered Finance documents were spread across 4 different storage systems (SharePoint, Drive, email archives, shared server). Took weeks to consolidate.
- **"Stakeholder Resistance: The Finance Lead's Skepticism"**: Why it took 2 weeks to convince Finance this mattered; how we eventually got buy-in.

---

## Key Takeaway

**Before you assess your organization against ISO 27001, know what you actually have.**

You don't have it until it's written down.

And writing it down, first time, from an organization that's never documented before, takes way longer than the framework lets you think.

Budget for it. It's step zero, not step three.
