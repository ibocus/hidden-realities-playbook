# Story: The Documentation Ghost

**Theme**: Legacy Documentation & Integration

**Date Captured**: 2026-08-15

**Status**: Raw

### The Assumption
We assumed most critical business processes would be documented somewhere in the organization.

### The Reality
During the first discovery workshop with Finance, we learned 40% of critical controls lived nowhere but in people's heads—spreadsheets, ad-hoc notes, tribal knowledge. The team lead said: "We know how to do it; it's just not written down."

### Why It Matters
Without documented procedures:
- We couldn't assess the control against ISO requirements
- No consistency across the team
- Audit would fail (no evidence the control exists)
- Completely dependent on one person
- Impossible to hand off or train new team members

### The Decision
We stopped the gap analysis for two weeks. Instead:
1. Sent Finance lead a template for 5 critical processes
2. Scheduled follow-up interviews
3. Built procedure documentation into monthly governance checklist
4. Assigned ownership to Finance team lead (not IT)

### The Lesson
**Discovery phase is first about documentation, not assessment.** Don't assume docs exist—assume they don't, and you'll be pleased when some do.

**Next time**: Before gap analysis, do a documentation audit. Budget discovery phase as 4-6 weeks, not 2, because you're writing docs first, assessing second.

### Time Investment
~17.5 hours (workshops + interviews + guidance creation)

### Context
- This wasn't unique to Finance. Happened in 5 other departments too.
- Total undocumented processes discovered: 47 across the org
- This became a 3-week detour in the project timeline

- "Format Harmonization Challenge: Templates from different teams didn't align"
- "Stakeholder Resistance: Why Finance didn't want to document informally"
