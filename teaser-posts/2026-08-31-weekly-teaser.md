## LinkedIn Draft

When I told people I was writing a book about ISO 27001, most assumed it would summarize the standard. Clause by clause, control by control.

Three stories into capturing this project in real time, and not one has been about a control. The first was about what language to write documentation in. The second was about where the documents should even live, and who gets to see the drafts before they're approved. The third was about getting a sponsor to actually sign the scope document, not just nod at it in a meeting.

None of that is written anywhere in the standard. ISO 27001 tells you what to produce. It stays silent on the decisions you have to make just to get there.

The pattern so far: most of what slows an implementation down isn't technical. It's organizational friction the standard assumes you'll quietly figure out on your own.

If you've implemented ISO 27001, or you're in the middle of it right now, what's the decision nobody warned you about? Send me a DM, I'm collecting these for the book.
