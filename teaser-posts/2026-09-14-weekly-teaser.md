## LinkedIn Draft

Nobody warns you about the decisions that show up before you write a single control.

Three months into building this ISMS, the surprises so far have nothing to do with risk assessments or Annex A. They're upstream of all that.

Before we drafted anything, we had to pick a documentation language, because the org's existing files were split between English and French with no logic behind it. Before that even settled, we had to decide where documents would live and who could see drafts versus approved versions, because an auditor should never see a redline. And before either of those, the scope document needed the sponsor's actual signature, not a nod in a meeting, because it's the first thing an auditor asks for.

None of this is in the standard. ISO 27001 assumes you already know where your files sit and that your sponsor already agrees with what "in scope" means.

Starting an implementation soon? Settle documentation location and access before you draft anything, get scope formally signed before you touch a control, and don't assume language is neutral if your org has any multilingual history.

What's the pre-control decision that caught you off guard? Send me a DM, still collecting these for the book.
