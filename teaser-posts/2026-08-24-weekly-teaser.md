## LinkedIn Draft

Most ISO 27001 guidance treats scope as an internal, technical exercise. Draft it, get a nod from leadership, start mapping controls.

That's not how it went for us. Before any implementation work could begin, the scope document had to be formally signed by the sponsor, not casually approved in a meeting. It's also the first document an auditor asks for. Every control and every piece of evidence after that gets measured against what it says.

So the harder question wasn't what to write. It was whether what we wrote genuinely matched what the sponsor thought they were getting. If those two things drift, and an auditor finds the gap before we do, that's a far worse conversation.

We walked the sponsor through the scope in plain terms before asking for a signature, checking it against what they actually expected the ISMS to cover, not just what was technically convenient to certify.

Scope isn't a document you write once and move past. It's the foundation the whole audit gets measured against, and the first person to test whether it says what everyone assumed is usually the auditor. Better to find any mismatch before that.

Heading into your own scope conversation soon? I'd like to hear how you're framing sponsor sign-off. Send me a DM.
