## LinkedIn Draft

I assumed a real implementation would hand me a new "hidden reality" every week, tidy enough for a post.

That is not how it went. Three stories landed inside the first ten days of capturing this project: which language to write documentation in, where the documents live and who can see the drafts, and getting the scope document actually signed by the sponsor. All three clustered right at the start, at the exact decision points nobody warns you about.

Then five weeks of nothing worth writing down. Not because the implementation stalled. Because the work moved into normal execution: workshops, drafting, more workshops. The organizational friction that makes a good story doesn't arrive on a schedule. It shows up at specific junctions and clusters there, then goes quiet.

If you're documenting a live project expecting a steady weekly supply of insight, recalibrate. Material comes in bursts around decisions, not a constant stream. Confusing the two clocks means you either pad with fluff or go quiet yourself.

If you've tried to capture a live project in real time, did your material cluster the same way, or come steadily? Send me a DM, curious whether this is universal or just us.
